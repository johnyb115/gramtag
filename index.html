Three steps at the stove

Gramtag does not plan your meals or tell you how much to eat. It takes the portions you already follow and does the maths after cooking.

  1. 1

    Set your portions once

    Raw grams per portion for each person and each food, straight from your meal plan.

  2. 2

    Cook and weigh

    Weigh all the cooked meat and side. In the pot is fine: Gramtag knows your pots and takes their weight off.

  3. 3

    Read each plate

    Each person's grams print on a tag, big enough to read from the counter while you serve.

Questions

How do I split cooked food fairly by weight?

Set each person's raw portion once. After cooking, weigh the whole pot: Gramtag divides the cooked weight in the same proportion as the raw portions, so each plate gets exactly its share.

Why does cooked meat weigh less than raw meat?

Meat loses water as it cooks, often around a quarter of its weight, while rice and pasta take water in. That is why raw portions from a meal plan cannot be weighed straight after cooking; Gramtag converts them for you.

Can I weigh the food in the pot?

Yes. Store each pot's empty weight once, switch on “Weighed in a pan”, and Gramtag takes the pot's weight off.

Does Gramtag make a meal plan for me?

No. It works with the portions you already follow, from your own plan or a nutritionist. It does not recommend amounts or give health advice.

Is there an iPhone version?

Gramtag runs in the browser at gramtag.eu/app, on an iPhone too; add it to the home screen to open it like an app. The Android app is in testing on Google Play.

Where is my data stored?

On your device. Nothing is sent anywhere unless you choose to share your freezer with another phone.
